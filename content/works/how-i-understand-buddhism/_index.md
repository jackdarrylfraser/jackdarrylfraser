These essays trace an attempt to align my understanding of Buddhism with the
values of science: careful attention to experience, causal explanation, and a
willingness to revise beliefs. That alignment does not mean asking science to
answer every kind of question. Science is powerful at explaining the empirical
world, but treating it as a source of final guidance about meaning, value, or
subjective expierence is a category error: those are not the questions its
methods are designed to settle. Science deals in descriptions and relations, not
being itself. It can model how systems perturb and interact with subjective
experience, but a model cannot embody the presence it describes—regardless of
whether matter precedes mind or mind precedes matter.

I find Buddhism a good fit in that space, not as a substitute for scientific
knowledge but as a set of traditions for examining experience, shaping conduct,
and working with questions science does not aim to resolve. The essays follow
that inquiry through experience, selfhood, conduct, and continuity.

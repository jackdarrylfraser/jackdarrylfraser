Vectors can be useful ways to describe direction and growth, but a site's tags
and writing may communicate them better than fixed categories.

<!-- excerpt -->

I have used the word *vector* for a deliberate direction of learning: a set of
ideas and practices that build on one another. The metaphor is useful. A vector
has direction, and a collection of writing can show how one question leads to
another.

And different from a goal, vectors seem to capture something different. 

Take for example the goal of growing old, having no regrets and being well rounded. It's not immeadietly clear to me that stackinf up a bunch of 90 day SMART goals one after another from now until you are 70 is how you arrive in old age with no regrets and being well rounded. 

There seems to be some tag along something that is associated with goal making that causes it be like the total sum of the parts is the equal to it sum of of it's whole.

I suppose you could say something similiar about vectors. That because you have defined starts and deadlines for SMART goals that they can give you a multiplicative return where you take 3-4 earlier goals and use those skills to work on something you couldn't have otherwise. 

And in the worst case a vector just allows you to be too implicit. You meanader along a path never noticing the patterns from the broken down modules of skill building only to under achieve. To me that misses something huge in this process. That ultimately if your goal is about not having regrets and being well rounded those are fundamentally perceptual. 

So I suppose what is more important, being less happy but trying to be structured enough to get more things done and accomplish more. Or being more focused on your self perception and esteem throughout the process?

I don't think you need to pick either of them in reality but clearly they both have different implications as ways to look at the progression of a life of interest pursuit.
